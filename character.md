# UNIVERSAL 4-VIEW CHARACTER SHEET — MASTER PROMPT v2

Two versions of the same prompt:
- **A. FULL MASTER PROMPT**: for models without a length limit (Gemini, ChatGPT, Midjourney with a long prompt, ComfyUI).
- **B. COMPACT PROMPT (< 5000 characters)**: for sites with a limit (seedance.tv, mage.space and similar).

Upload your image as the reference (`@image1`) and paste one of the versions.

---

## A. FULL MASTER PROMPT

```
UNIVERSAL 4-VIEW CHARACTER SHEET — MASTER PROMPT

1. REFERENCE IMAGE — ABSOLUTE SUBJECT PRESERVATION
Use the uploaded reference image as the sole source of truth for the subject's identity, appearance, design and physical characteristics. The subject may be a human, fictional character, animal, bird, insect, mythical creature, robot, toy, vehicle, product, everyday object or any other entity. First identify the subject type, then adapt the presentation to it while preserving the original subject exactly as shown.

STRICT PRESERVATION RULES:
- Preserve the exact recognizable identity and original design of the subject.
- Keep the same colors, markings, patterns, textures, materials, proportions and distinctive features.
- Humans and characters: preserve the exact face (face shape, jawline, chin, cheekbones, eye shape and spacing, eyelids, brows, nose, lips, ears), hairstyle, hair length, hair color, skin tone, freckles, moles, scars, body type, height impression, age appearance and clothing.
- Animals and creatures: preserve species, breed features, fur, feathers or scales, markings, eye color and shape, anatomy and proportions.
- Objects, toys, robots and vehicles: preserve shape, silhouette, dimensions, colors, materials, surface finish, wear, components, accessories and construction.
- Preserve every visible garment, collar, seam, pocket, zipper, button, jewelry piece, piece of equipment, prop, label and accessory exactly as shown.
- Do not redesign, beautify, slim, de-age, stylize, replace or "improve" the subject.
- Do not add features, accessories, patterns, logos or details absent from the reference.
- If a part is hidden in the reference, continue it logically from what is visible; never invent a conflicting design.
The output must depict the same exact subject, not a similar-looking alternative.

2. FIXED FOUR-PANEL LAYOUT
One horizontal 16:9 image, divided into four vertical panels of exactly equal width, separated by thin, perfectly straight, clean seams. Left to right:

PANEL 1 — EXTREME CLOSE-UP (85mm portrait lens, f/2.8, focus on the eyes)
Head and shoulders, framed from the upper chest up, subject looking straight into the lens. If the subject has no face, show its most recognizable front detail. This panel is the master identity reference for the other three panels.

PANEL 2 — FRONT VIEW (50mm lens, f/5.6, camera at chest height)
The complete subject from directly in front, head to toe, natural relaxed neutral standing pose, arms resting at the sides, weight evenly on both feet. Objects and vehicles in their natural front orientation.

PANEL 3 — THREE-QUARTER VIEW (50mm lens, f/5.6, same camera height)
The complete subject turned about 45 degrees, revealing depth, volume and side structure, with the same pose and the same design. The face in this panel must match panel 1 exactly in shape and proportion.

PANEL 4 — BACK VIEW (50mm lens, f/5.6, same camera height)
The complete subject seen directly from behind: real back anatomy, back of the head and hair, back seams, yokes, pockets, markings, fur pattern, materials and components. Never mirror or copy the front onto the back.

In panels 2, 3 and 4 the subject has the same height, the same scale, the same head position and the same foot line, with a small margin above the head and below the feet. Nothing is cropped.

3. COMPOSITION & BACKGROUND
- One seamless mid-grey paper studio backdrop with a soft natural floor gradient, identical in tone and brightness in all four panels.
- Soft, realistic contact shadows under the feet, paws, wheels or base in every full-body panel; the subject stands firmly on the floor, never floating.
- No borders, frames, text, labels, numbers, logos, watermarks, props or extra subjects.
- Clean, premium, professional reference-sheet composition.

4. LIGHTING & CAMERA
Professional studio photography. One large soft key light from front-left at about 45 degrees, a gentle fill light from the right, a subtle rim light from behind separating hair, shoulders and edges from the background. Identical lighting setup in all four panels. Full-frame digital camera, ISO 100, tack-sharp focus, natural depth of field, accurate perspective with no lens distortion in the full-body panels.

5. PHOTOREALISM — MICRO DETAIL
The image must look like a real photograph, not a render.
- Skin: real macro texture with visible pores on the nose, cheeks and forehead, fine lines under the eyes, natural uneven tone, subtle redness around the nose, eyelids and lips, tiny natural imperfections, vellus hair on the cheeks and jaw catching the light, natural lip texture, subsurface scattering in the ears, nose tip and fingertips. Faces in the full-body panels are as sharp as the close-up: crisp catchlights in both eyes, defined lashes, visible skin texture.
- Eyes: wet, reflective, with detailed irises, natural sclera with faint veins, real catchlights from the key light.
- Hair: individual strands, natural density at the parting and crown, flyaway hairs against the background, baby hairs at the hairline, realistic shine that follows the hair's shape.
- Hands and feet: correct anatomy, five clearly separated fingers and toes, natural nails, knuckles, tendons and veins, natural creases.
- Fabric: real weave and knit texture, correct seams and stitching, realistic weight and folds at the elbows, waist and knees, natural fading and wear only where fabric really rubs (seams, edges, cuffs, pocket openings, elbows, knees). Wear and dirt follow the folds and the weave; never flat painted smudges or powder.
- Fur, feathers, scales: individual hairs, barbs or scales with natural direction, density and sheen.
- Materials of objects: physically accurate metal, plastic, glass, rubber and paint, with real reflections, micro-scratches and edge wear.
- Wet or sweaty skin, if present in the reference: thin, uneven sheen with small sharp highlights and matte areas in between, small clear droplets with real refraction; never oily or gel-like.

6. COLOR
Neutral, true-to-reference color balance with no color cast. True greys stay grey (not green, brown or blue), true blacks stay deep but not crushed, natural skin tones with a healthy subtle pink in the lips, ears and nose. Very subtle fine film grain, crisp natural micro-contrast, no HDR look, no oversaturation, no oversharpening halos.

7. CONTINUITY LOCK — CRITICAL
All four panels show one and the same subject from different angles, as if photographed in one studio session within one minute. Absolute consistency in: identity and face, hairstyle, shape, proportions, height and silhouette, colors, markings, textures and materials, clothing details (collars, seams, pockets, zippers, buttons), accessories and components, age, anatomy and every distinctive feature of the reference. No variations, no alternate outfits, no different expression that alters identity, no different breed, no replacement parts.

8. NEGATIVE / AVOID
Changed identity, different face between panels, wider or masculine or older face in profile, beautified or slimmed face, blurry small faces, plastic, waxy or airbrushed skin, beauty filter, doll skin, CGI, 3D render, illustration, painting, anime, cartoon, deformed or extra fingers, fused fingers, malformed toes, flat or oversized feet, floating subject, missing floor shadows, cropped head or feet, mirrored front on the back, different collar or clothing details between panels, see-through or mesh fabric not present in the reference, painted or powdery dirt, gel-like droplets, oily skin, color cast, uneven or brighter panels, unequal panel widths, borders, text, logos, watermark, extra people or objects.

9. FINAL OUTPUT
One clean, photorealistic, four-panel reference sheet of the uploaded subject, consistent in every panel, suitable as a reference for later AI image generation, animation and cinematic video production.

RESOLUTION: render at the highest native resolution the model supports, exact 16:9 (for example 3840x2160 or 2560x1440), with maximum detail; the image is intended for later upscaling.

FINAL PRIORITY: exact reference preservation > identical face and design across all four panels > correct anatomy and construction > photorealistic micro detail > lighting and color > artistic enhancement.
```

## What changed compared to the original prompt

- **Lenses and camera height for every panel** (85mm for the close-up, 50mm for the others, same height). This takes away the "different face in profile" effect and the distortion.
- **The close-up is the identity master.** Panels 2 and 3 have to copy that face, which fixes the wider, more masculine profile.
- **A "micro detail" section** covering skin, eyes, hair, hands, feet, fabric, fur and materials. These are exactly the places where models produce plastic or smudge things.
- **Fixes for the errors from your test runs.** The negatives now explicitly cover a mesh t-shirt, "painted" dirt, gel-like droplets, floating feet, a brighter panel, a different collar and a colour cast.
- **The same height and foot line in panels 2–4** plus shadows under the feet, so the figures don't float or jump around in scale.
- **Colour.** Greys stay neutral grey, with no green or brown cast.
- **Resolution.** The 16K line is gone: no current image model outputs 15360×8640, and that kind of requirement only confuses it. Generate at the model's maximum (2K–4K), then upscale separately to 4K, 8K or higher with an upscaler. This gives much more real detail than "16K" in a prompt.
- **A compact version under 5000 characters** for sites with a length limit.
